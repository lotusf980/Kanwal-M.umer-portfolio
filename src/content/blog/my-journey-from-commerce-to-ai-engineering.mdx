I started my academic journey in Commerce, but over time I became increasingly interested in technology, programming, and Artificial Intelligence.

My transition from Commerce to AI Engineering has been a gradual learning journey. I started learning programming and later moved toward AI and Agentic AI through GIAIC.

One of the most important parts of this journey has been learning by building. Instead of focusing only on theory, I have worked on practical projects that helped me understand how software and AI systems are actually developed.

My projects have included full-stack applications, AI-related projects, documentation websites, and experiments with modern AI technologies.

One of my major projects is a documentation-based book about Physical AI and Humanoid Robotics. The project uses modern web technologies and explores topics such as ROS 2, simulation, robotics, and Vision-Language-Action systems.

I have also explored Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), vector databases, APIs, and AI-powered question-answering systems.

My Commerce background is also an important part of my journey. It gives me a different perspective when thinking about software and AI products because I am interested not only in how technology works, but also in the business problems that technology can solve.

This combination of business understanding and technology has influenced my long-term goal.

I want to become an AI Engineer who can understand a real-world problem, design an appropriate technical solution, and build a useful product around it.

Currently, I am focusing on AI Engineering, Agentic AI, full-stack development, and practical AI applications.

I am especially interested in AI agents that can work with tools, follow workflows, retrieve information, and help automate real business processes.

The journey has not always been easy. Moving from a Commerce background into programming and AI requires continuous learning and practice.

There are many technologies to understand, and the field changes quickly. However, every project has helped me learn something new.

One of the biggest lessons I have learned is that learning technology becomes much more meaningful when you build something with it.

A project exposes problems that tutorials often do not show. Debugging, architecture, APIs, databases, deployment, user experience, and integration all become part of the learning process.

My goal is not simply to learn AI technologies.

I want to use those technologies to build practical products that solve real problems.

My transition from Commerce to AI Engineering is still a work in progress, but every project is helping me move closer to that goal.
